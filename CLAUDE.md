# MowgliNext

Open-source autonomous robot mower monorepo. ROS2 Lyrical (Ubuntu 26.04 containers), Nav2, and a GTSAM iSAM2 factor-graph localizer (`fusion_graph`, in `ros2/src/fusion_graph/`) that is the **sole and default** map+odom localizer — it owns **both** `map→odom` AND `odom→base_footprint` and can use an RTK-built LiDAR map anchor after a complete GNSS outage. **The robot_localization dual-EKF (`ekf_map_node` + `ekf_odom_node`), `config/robot_localization.yaml`, slam_toolbox, Kinematic-ICP, scan-to-scan ICP, and loop closure were all removed**; `navigation.launch.py` launches `fusion_graph_node` unconditionally (there is no `use_fusion_graph` arg). BehaviorTree.CPP v4, multi-area continuous-subpath coverage. (`docs/HANDOFF_FUSION_GRAPH.md` is historical — the migration record, **not** the steady-state reference; see [`wiki/Architecture.md`](https://github.com/mowglinext/mowglinext/wiki/Architecture#optional-factor-graph-localizer-fusion_graph) for current behaviour.) **Drive base:** the wheel-velocity loop AND the yaw-rate loop both run in STM32 firmware (vendored PX4 PID per-wheel + a gyro-based differential yaw-rate loop, Option C, 2026-07-17). ROS2 sends `cmd_vel` straight through with no host-side shaping — `hardware_bridge_node`'s host-side closed-loop angular-rate PI (`angular_rate_controller.hpp`, Option B) was removed; firmware closes both loops on the wire-level command.

**Website:** https://mowgli.garden | **Wiki:** https://github.com/mowglinext/mowglinext/wiki | **First-boot checklist:** [`docs/FIRST_BOOT.md`](docs/FIRST_BOOT.md)

Lyrical migration, pinned source dependencies and acceptance procedure: [`docs/ROS2_LYRICAL_MIGRATION.md`](docs/ROS2_LYRICAL_MIGRATION.md). Historical Kilted hardware observations do not validate Lyrical.

## Safety — READ FIRST

This robot has spinning blades. The STM32 firmware is the sole blade safety authority.

- NEVER bypass firmware blade safety checks from ROS2
- Blade commands from ROS2 are fire-and-forget — firmware decides whether to execute
- Emergency stop is handled by firmware, not software
- Flag ANY change that could affect physical behavior as safety-critical in PR reviews

## Architecture Invariants (DO NOT VIOLATE)

1. **Localizer = `fusion_graph` (GTSAM iSAM2), sole and default.** `fusion_graph_node` is launched unconditionally by `navigation.launch.py` and publishes **both** `map→odom` AND `odom→base_footprint` (replacing the removed `ekf_map_node` + `ekf_odom_node`). There is **no `use_fusion_graph` launch arg** — do not write it from the GUI. It runs a Pose2 graph (`node_period_s` cadence) with a custom `GnssLeverArmFactor` (analytic Jacobian — antenna lever-arm rotates with the node's yaw), a wheel between-factor with non-holonomic covariance (σ_y << σ_x), a gyro between-factor on yaw, and COG/mag yaw unary factors. Inputs: **`/gps/fix` (raw `sensor_msgs/NavSatFix`) — subscribed DIRECTLY** (`fusion_graph_node_setup_comms.cpp`), with the datum + lever-arm projection done inside the node (`LatLonToMap` + `GnssLeverArmFactor`, datum read from `mowgli_robot.yaml`); plus `/imu/cog_heading` (GPS COG yaw, `cog_to_imu_node`) and `/imu/mag_yaw` (when calibrated). It does **NOT** subscribe to `/gps/pose_cov` — that topic (from `navsat_to_absolute_pose_node`) is consumed by `map_server_node` as the RTK-quality gate for `~/set_docking_point`, and by the GUI/BT. RTK-Fixed σ ~3 mm flows through as-is. `OnGnss` runs two independent payload-staleness backstops against a receiver that can keep delivering fresh receipt stamps while its solved position stops moving (mowglinext#694), because `gnss_observation_tracker_`'s own receipt-stamp dedup cannot tell that apart from a genuine new observation. First, it subscribes to `/mowgli/localization/mode_id` (`localization_monitor_node`) as a trust gate: while it reports DEAD_RECKONING, `/gps/fix` samples are withheld exactly like a genuine outage (`GraphStats::gps_rejects_dead_reckoning`) — `localization_monitor_node` pairs `/gps/fix` with `/gps/status`'s typed `position_observation_sequence` to compute this, a pairing deliberately not duplicated a second time inside `fusion_graph` itself (deferred as MGNSS-002; `/gps/fix` stays the one direct subscription). **Field data proved this alone is insufficient**: two full mowing sessions (2026-09-20) showed `/gps/fix` frozen bit-for-bit at one lat/lon for the entire session while the robot moved 2.7-4.5 m, yet `localization_monitor_node` never once reported DEAD_RECKONING — the receiver's `position_observation_sequence` apparently advances on its own internal acceptance cadence, not on the solved position actually changing, so sequence-based pairing is blind to this exact failure. Second, and load-bearing: a direct payload-VALUE comparison (`gps_stuck_gate.hpp`) tracks wheel distance accumulated since `msg->latitude/longitude` last actually differed from the previous sample, and withholds the sample once that exceeds `gps_stuck_min_wheel_dist_m` (1.0 m default, standing down above `gps_stuck_max_yaw_rad` mid-turn) — counted separately as `GraphStats::gps_rejects_stuck_value` so diagnostics can tell which mechanism actually fired. **LiDAR uses scan-to-map only** (`use_lidar_map_anchor`, template default `true`, ANDed with `use_lidar` in navigation; standalone node opt-in). The node no longer computes ICP scan-between factors, ICP loop closures, or ICP boot relocalization; their flags, parameters and scan-storage paths are removed. Historic graph pose files remain readable. Under fresh RTK-Fixed, decimated scans teach georeferenced persistent tiles (`<graph_save_prefix>.lidartiles`), with only a local window loaded (default 5 × 5 tiles of 10 m, resolution 0.1 m). `/fusion_graph/lidar_map` publishes that moving local window in map coordinates. Disk work is asynchronous; legacy `.lidarmap` files migrate with datum/resolution validation. The Beluga PF runs at most 5 Hz, in 10 s calibration bursts every 60 s under Fixed. Any usable GNSS observation, including RTK Float, keeps it asleep; after a complete GNSS outage it starts at 15 s to warm up before XY factors become eligible at 20 s. Likelihood-field rebuilding is deferred until computation. Each candidate still passes scan support, particle spread, dead-reckoning plausibility and acquisition freshness gates; tiles do not create features on an empty lawn. The XY factor accounts for acquisition-to-node offset and never supplies absolute yaw. `lidar_anchor_shadow_mode` enables continuous bounded-rate calibration under Fixed. Surface: `/fusion_graph/diagnostics` (1 Hz `DiagnosticArray`: `total_nodes / lidar_anchor_updates / lidar_filter_calls / cov_xx|yy|yawyaw`), `/fusion_graph/markers`, `/imu/fg_yaw`, services `~/save_graph` + `~/clear_graph` (`std_srvs/Trigger`, in the GUI's Diagnostics → Fusion Graph panel).

   **slam_toolbox, Kinematic-ICP, and FusionCore are gone.** The previous RTK-fallback architecture (parallel TF tree, `slam_pose_anchor_node` EWMA, `wheel_odom_tf_node`, `slam_scan_frame_relay`, `slam_map_persist_node`, `rtk_pose_mux_node`, fusion as `pose1`), the K-ICP encoder adapter, and the FusionCore 22D UKF were all removed in favor of the factor-graph approach. `fusion_graph` is the only LiDAR-aware localizer; the `kinematic_icp` and `fusioncore` submodules are no longer part of the build.
2. **TF chain follows REP-105** — `map → odom → base_footprint → base_link → sensors`. **Both `map→odom` AND `odom→base_footprint` are published by `fusion_graph_node`** (the `ekf_map_node`/`ekf_odom_node` that used to own them are removed). Do NOT publish either TF from any other node. All Nav2 nodes use `base_footprint` as the robot frame. `base_link` is at the rear wheel axis (OpenMower convention, do not move).
3. **Cyclone DDS** — not FastRTPS (stale shm issues on ARM)
4. **Map frame = GPS frame** — X=east, Y=north, no rotation transform. All persisted geometry (areas.dat polygons, dock_pose) is anchored ONLY by `datum_lat`/`datum_lon`; `map_server_node` stamps the datum into `areas.dat` and, on a datum change, re-projects the whole map + dock pose into the new frame at load (issue #216, `mowgli_interfaces/wgs84_projection.hpp` — same equirectangular math as the localizer). Do NOT strip the `datum_lat`/`datum_lon` stamp from areas.dat or hand map_server a datum different from navsat's.
5. **Coverage is never replanned around costmap obstacles** — the F2C route is driven as planned. With LiDAR, obstacles are handled by FTC deviating the path LATERALLY in real time (`enable_obstacle_deviation`, reading the LOCAL costmap `obstacle_layer` — **a lethal cell there is an obstacle, with nothing subtracted**), with collision_monitor as the real-time guard. **The deviation is planned as a whole PROFILE** (`use_offset_lattice`, `ftc_offset_lattice.hpp`): a (station × lateral offset) lattice over `avoidance_horizon_m`, searched by dynamic programming with DWB-style separate critics — but in PATH space, so FTC's PID still tracks and nothing samples velocities (that is what got MPPI reverted). The legacy search (ONE offset for the whole window, side picked from a single pose and latched) stays as a live rollback; on the 2026-09-17 bag it logged 117 × WEDGED where the lattice found a 0.20–0.30 m skirt. **A TURN the lattice cannot drive is improvised, not aborted** (turn fallback, `mowgli_nav2_plugins/ftc_turn_fallback.hpp`, `turn_fallback_*` in the FTC block): a lateral offset cannot shorten a turn, and on 2026-09-22 all 8 WEDGED of a 110-min mow were the body front meeting real lethal cells at a U-turn, omega loop or 70–107° kink — 7× the hedge just past the recorded line where the body overhangs it at a row end (see the soft band below), 1× an unmapped object beside a drawn obstacle. Where the lattice finds no profile AND the first body-blocked plan pose lies in a turn (plan heading sweep ≥ 45°), FTC (bounded straight reverse, ≤ 0.40 m, only with `obstacle_reverse_enabled`) pivots, drives a straight connector, pivots, and rejoins the plan past the turn — the rejoin that skips the least path within 3 m, planned with the executing checks (pivot sweep = real footprint vs true-lethal cells; connector and rejoin = the lattice body; body axis vs the zone off the plan) plus a 0.05 m margin, and EXECUTED by the existing PIVOT state on runtime pivot corners spliced into FTC's plan, so every cycle re-checks it and anything new stops it (hold → abort). An obstacle on a straight, or a turn with no safe way round, takes the unchanged WEDGED → reverse-escape → hold → abort path. Like the lateral skirt it is a bounded LOCAL deviation: the route is not replanned, and the goal checker / FollowStrip cursor are handed the remainder from the rejoin (`/controller_server/FollowCoveragePath/global_plan`). The global keepout costmap FTC also holds is used for CONFINEMENT ONLY (`confine_deviation_to_zone` rejects lateral OFFSET candidates that leave the zone). Its second, subtractive use — `ignore_obstacles_outside_zone`, the issue-#517 zone mask, where a lethal local cell that was ALSO lethal globally counted as no obstacle — was REMOVED 2026-09-17: `keepout_filter` stamps every DRAWN obstacle lethal globally, so "the operator mapped this tree" erased "the LiDAR can see this tree" and FTC drove into the same mapped tree twice on 2026-09-16. Edge mowing is protected instead by map_server's NON-LETHAL soft band (`kSoftPenaltyMaskCost` 50 over `enforce_boundary_margin_m`, floored at `robot_config_util.chassis_circumscribed_radius` — 0.597 m shipped — in `full_system.launch.py`; the band must hold the whole footprint, which reaches 0.275 m sideways but **0.53 m forward** at a row end): with `chassis_safety_inset` 0 the outermost pass rides ON the recorded line and the body overhangs it, over cells below FTC's threshold. In the no-LiDAR overlay both FTC obstacle flags (`check_obstacles`, `enable_obstacle_deviation`) are OFF and collision_monitor is pass-through. `keepout_filter` is a GLOBAL-costmap plugin only — it is deliberately NOT in the local costmap, where it made the recorded boundary lethal and tripped "collision ahead" on edge swaths. **In the global costmap it is listed AFTER `inflation_layer` in BOTH overlays (`[obstacle_layer|static_layer, inflation_layer, keepout_filter]`), so the keepout mask — recorded boundary and drawn obstacles — is NOT inflated; only real sensor marks are.** The mask already carries the body (see the "count the body exactly once" bullet below); the old order inflated it a second time and made a robot on its own coverage line un-plannable (37 s transit timeouts, a first headland skipped, START_OCCUPIED beside drawn obstacles). Hand-applied on the robot 2026-09-17 (17-minute mow, zero START_OCCUPIED, zero boundary e-stops) and pinned by `test_nav2_params.py::test_global_costmap_inflates_before_the_keepout_filter`.
6. **dock_pose + tool_width live in `mowgli_robot.yaml`** — single source of truth. `dock_pose_x/y/yaw`: exactly ONE file calls `robot_yaml_scalar::UpdateDockPose` (line-splice updates that preserve comments) — `mowgli_map/src/area_manager.cpp`, i.e. `map_server_node`'s `/set_docking_point` ROS service (the ONE canonical persist writer) plus its datum-migration re-splice. `calibrate_imu_yaw_node` no longer writes the file itself: BOTH its one-click dock calibration and its legacy `~/calibrate` dock pre-phase persist through `/set_docking_point`. **A MEASURED dock position always comes from the RAW GPS antenna (`/gps/fix` projected with the datum), lever-arm-corrected ONCE (`mowgli_map/dock_antenna_capture.hpp`) with the yaw being persisted — never from the fused pose, `/gps/absolute_pose` or `/gps/pose_cov`**: while charging, `fusion_graph` gauge-resets the fused pose onto the STORED dock pose and those topics are lever-arm-corrected with that pinned yaw, so on the dock they are circular and re-save the old value. The calibration persists in ONE write, off the dock: on the dock, charging and RTK-Fixed (the one moment all three hold — it already waits for RTK-Fixed there before moving) it has map_server capture the antenna (`~/capture_dock_antenna`, held in memory, single-use, `dock_antenna_capture_ttl_s` 300 s); after the reverse leg it sends `use_pending_antenna` + `yaw_source=MOTION` and map_server writes x, y and yaw together. No capture → `preserve_position` + `MOTION`, yaw only. Those two MOTION requests are the ONLY ones `mowgli_map/dock_set_gates.hpp` exempts from the `is_charging` gate (the yaw only exists ~1.5 m off the dock), and neither lets the caller supply a position. The re-dock afterwards is a pure CONFIRMATION pass: `docking_server` / `gps_dock_detection` load `dock_pose` at stack startup only, so it steers on the OLD pose and may stop short if that was wrong — not a calibration failure. Do NOT move the position capture back after the re-dock (circular: a wrong stored position makes the re-dock stop short of the contacts, so the capture that would fix it is never reached — field test 2026-09-17, 0.35 m short), and do NOT send a live `use_gps_position` capture from off the dock (correctly rejected; it made every calibration fail the same day). `calibration_nodes.cpp` no longer writes the file at all — its per-undock EMA writeback was removed so there is exactly one canonical writer; `CalibrateHeadingFromUndock` keeps only a runtime `~/set_pose` seed. `hardware_bridge`, `map_server`, and legacy-inlined dock-pose logic read the values as ROS parameters at startup. There is no `dock_calibration.yaml` or separate `dock_yaw_to_set_pose` node anymore. `tool_width` (effective blade cut width): both derive from the ONE `tool_width` scalar in `mowgli_robot.yaml`, but via two different launch files and NOT as numerically identical values. `full_system.launch.py` flows it straight into `map_server.tool_width` (mark_cells_mowed stamp radius + sliver detection). `navigation.launch.py` derives `coverage_server.operation_width` (F2C `Robot::setCovWidth`, controls swath spacing) as `tool_width − swath_overlap` (`swath_overlap` default 0.02 m, also from `mowgli_robot.yaml`) — adjacent F2C swaths deliberately OVERLAP by that amount; without it the headland↔first-swath seam and BruteForce's edge margins still left thin un-mowed bands even with `operation_width == tool_width`. Both launch files' fallback default for `tool_width` itself is single-sourced from `robot_config_util.DEFAULT_TOOL_WIDTH_M` (`ros2/src/mowgli_bringup/launch/robot_config_util.py`), not independently hardcoded — that independent-hardcoding is exactly what caused the original bug: the two used to be configured completely separately (`mower_width=0.18` + a static `operation_width=0.20`), which left thin un-mowed strips between adjacent F2C swaths because map_server's stamp radius was narrower than F2C's spacing.
7. **Multi-area coverage = explicit segments from `plan_coverage` (F2C v3, no turn planning).** `map_server_node` owns area polygons and the `mow_progress` grid layer. BT outer loop `GetNextUnmowedArea` iterates areas; per area, `PlanCoverageArea` calls `map_server_node/get_mowing_area` (outer ring + obstacle holes) and forwards it to `mowgli_coverage`'s `plan_coverage` action (`mowgli_interfaces/action/PlanCoverage`, Fields2Cover v3 at `/opt/fields2cover-300`). The result carries BOTH an ordered list of discrete straight segments — concentric headland rings (outermost first, each split into corner-to-corner arcs) then straight serpentine swaths, kept for the GUI and resume bookkeeping — AND one or more continuous, **hole-free** `drivable_subpaths` (`buildContinuousSubPaths`) that join the rings + swaths with **forward turn-around arcs** of nominal radius `connector_turn_radius` (default 0.20 m **hardcoded in `navigation.launch.py`** — the one coverage knob with NO entry in the `mowgli_bringup` template, an Invariant 15 gap; a key in the installed `mowgli_robot.yaml` still overrides it, and the injected value is clamped to `[max(min_turning_radius, 0.10), 0.50]`) — cusp-free, bounded to the chassis-safety inset. A hole-free field yields ONE sub-path per run of joins that stay continuous: since `40d0c30b` a straight fallback join is only kept blade-on as a straight when it is tangent to both neighbours within 15°. Since 2026-09-21 (PR #716) a non-tangent join that fits no arc is kept blade-on as a **PIVOT JOIN** instead of splitting, when it is ≤ 0.6 m (`kSegmentTransitGapM`), passes the same boundary/hole checks as any join, and the disc the body sweeps pivoting about the rear axle (`pivot_sweep_radius` = `chassis_circumscribed_radius`) stays inside the recorded boundary + the soft band (`boundary_soft_margin`, same floor map_server gets) and clear of every drawn obstacle. A pivot corner is TWO consecutive poses at the SAME position, incoming heading then outgoing heading, turning > 15° (`mowgli_interfaces/coverage_geometry.hpp` — no .msg change); a zero-length step appears nowhere else. Both params are injected by `navigation.launch.py`; the server default 0.0 disables pivots. Only joins that fail those checks (next to drawn obstacles, across concave bites, real relocations) and hole gaps split the sub-path — the 2026-09-21 lawn at AUTO rings went from 135 sub-paths to 10; a multi-hole field is split so each obstacle gap becomes a blade-off Nav2 transit, and the sub-paths are ordered by a deterministic greedy nearest-neighbour pass to minimise that blade-off transit. `FollowStrip` drives each sub-path as ONE continuous `FollowCoveragePath` goal (FTCController tracks it end-to-end — no per-segment dispatch), bridging consecutive sub-paths with a blade-off NavigateToPose transit that routes around the obstacle, and resumes mid-area by trimming at a pose cursor into the sub-path concatenation (plan is deterministic so the cursor is stable across re-plans). `full_path` is the concatenation, kept for the GUI. Too-small areas are rejected by the server (`success=false`), not by a BT-side size gate. **The headland ring stage is SKIPPABLE:** `num_headland_passes` is a three-way sentinel — `< 0` plans ZERO rings, `0` still means auto (`ceil(headland_width/op_width)`, floored at 1), `> 0` still forces the count. With ZERO rings the serpentine swaths become the outermost driven pass, and F2C insets the outermost swath by `op_width/2` exactly as it insets ring 0 (`sx[0] = 0.5*cov_width` vs `buffer(-w*(i+0.5))` — same convention, two generators), so the `field_offset = chassis_safety_inset − op_width/2` term applies in BOTH branches; it is only floored at 0 with the rings off (swath ENDS are clipped to the planning cell and must never cross the recorded line, a head-on direction `chassis_safety_inset` does not model). Net, and do not conflate the three distances: the outermost swath CENTERLINE lands on the same line ring 0 would have driven, so the uncut LATERAL band is unchanged (~0.12 m at stock config) — sideways "None" mows neither closer to the edge nor further. The swath ENDS, however, reach that band, so at a ROW END the robot noses ~op_width/2 closer to the boundary than it ever does with rings on, head-on, where `chassis_safety_inset`'s half-chassis-width reasoning does not apply. That is the cost of having no apron. The connector clearance ring is then `safe_boundary` EXACTLY (no outward slack — an outward expansion would let a turn centerline cross the recorded line under the server's 0.05 m verify slack); on-edge swath ends are accepted by `allInside()`'s 1 mm `kOnEdgeTolM` tolerance instead, and with no mowed apron most row-end U-turn arcs legitimately fall back to a straight pivot-through join (which still passes `allInside`; non-tangent ones became split sub-paths until PR #716 turned the short ones into pivot joins — the 5-pass headland apron dates from that split era and is no longer what keeps few-ring plans continuous). With the rings off, drawn obstacles grown by `obstacle_margin` can overlap each other and the boundary; the planning polygon is rebuilt as boundary minus obstacles there (the unrepaired polygon crashed the planner inside GEOS). It is read once at `coverage_server` startup, so a GUI change needs a stack restart.
8. **FTCController for coverage; RotationShim+RPP for transit.** The `FollowCoveragePath` slot is `mowgli_nav2_plugins/FTCController` — a deterministic Follow-the-Carrot controller with a decoupled lon/lat/ang PID (low-pass on the derivative). **RESTORED 2026-06-19**, reverting an MPPI experiment. FTC does ONE in-place `PRE_ROTATE` pivot to the path-start heading, then tracks the continuous `full_path` tightly (following the forward turn-around arcs), and at every planner PIVOT corner (Invariant 7) caps the carrot at the corner, stops within 2 cm, and rotates in place (`PIVOT` state, PRE_ROTATE's control and limits, `pivot_angle_tolerance_deg` 10°, abort at `goal_timeout`; a LiDAR swept-footprint check holds and then aborts if the rotation would hit something; no lateral deviation runs through a corner), and skirts obstacles by deviating the path LATERALLY in real time (`enable_obstacle_deviation`, reading the LiDAR `obstacle_layer`; off in the no-LiDAR overlay), and improvises a TURN the skirt cannot drive (turn fallback: reverse / pivot / straight / pivot / rejoin, executed by its PIVOT state — see Invariant 5). MPPI was reverted because as a SAMPLING controller it cut corners / made omega-loops at swath U-turns, and every attempt to sharpen its corners made it weave on the straights. The `FollowPath` (transit) slot uses RotationShim wrapping `nav2_regulated_pure_pursuit_controller` (RPP). **Nav2 params are a shared base + thin overlays:** `nav2_params_base.yaml` (everything common) is deep-merged with `nav2_params_lidar.yaml` OR `nav2_params_no_lidar.yaml` (only the genuine differences — costmap obstacle vs static layers, scan-based vs pass-through collision_monitor, `FollowPath.use_collision_detection`), selected by the `use_lidar` arg in `navigation.launch.py`. Edit shared params in the **base**; variant params in the **overlay**. `test_nav2_params.py` validates the merged result and pins the variants in lockstep.
9. **Emergency auto-reset on dock** — When emergency is active and robot is on dock (charging detected), BT auto-sends `ResetEmergency` to firmware. Firmware is sole safety authority and only clears latch if physical trigger is no longer asserted.
10. **Undock via Nav2 BackUp behavior** — BackUp (`undock_distance` 1.5 m, `undock_speed` 0.16 m/s — template defaults in `mowgli_robot.yaml`, injected into `behavior_tree_node` and read from the blackboard by `main_tree.xml`) via `behavior_server`, not `opennav_docking` UndockRobot (isDocked() unreliable with GPS drift near the dock). Costmaps are cleared after undock.
11. **Zero-odom only when charging** — `hardware_bridge_node` forces wheel and yaw velocity to zero whenever `is_charging_` is true, to prevent stationary-dock compass drift from corrupting the fused heading. The charger bit may briefly stay high during BackUp undock (~100 ms), but zeroing during that interval is harmless.
12. **Battery current for dock detection** — Hardware bridge publishes `abs(charging_current)` when charging, `0.0` when not, for `SimpleChargingDock` compatibility.
13. **Docking server cmd_vel** — Remapped to `/cmd_vel_docking` through twist_mux (priority 15).
14. **Coverage grid_map → OccupancyGrid convention** — easy to get wrong. `grid_map::GridMap::getSize()(0)` = cells along X, `getSize()(1)` = cells along Y. grid_map iterates with `r=0 → X_max` (decreasing) and `c=0 → Y_max` (decreasing). `nav_msgs/OccupancyGrid` has `width = X_cells`, `height = Y_cells`, row-major with `data[y_row * width + x_col]`, where `col=0 ↔ origin.x` (X_min) and `row=0 ↔ origin.y` (Y_min). To convert a grid_map cell `(r, c)` to an OccupancyGrid index: `og_col = nx - 1 - r`, `og_row = ny - 1 - c`, `flat_idx = og_row * nx + og_col`. ALWAYS set `mask.info.width = nx` and `mask.info.height = ny` — swapping produces a 90°-rotated mask, which silently marks valid interior polygon cells as lethal and breaks Smac planning with "Start occupied" errors. See `mow_progress_to_occupancy_grid()` in `map_server_node.cpp` as the reference implementation.
15. **Robot config is a SPARSE installed file over a TEMPLATE of defaults.** The *installed* `mowgli_robot.yaml` (`/ros2_ws/config/mowgli_robot.yaml`, seeded from `install/config/mowgli/mowgli_robot.yaml`) is **sparse** — it holds ONLY install-time choices (datum, NTRIP, `lidar_enabled`, GNSS hardware, `mower_model`) + calibration outputs (dock_pose, `ticks_per_meter`, `wheel_pid_*`, `imu_yaw`, mag). **Every other parameter's DEFAULT lives in the in-package template `ros2/src/mowgli_bringup/config/mowgli_robot.yaml`.** At launch, `ros2/src/mowgli_bringup/launch/robot_config_util.py` (`load_robot_params`) **DEEP-MERGES the sparse installed config OVER the template**, so an absent key falls through to its template default and nodes always receive a complete parameter set. Consequences: a maintainer changing a template default propagates to every robot that hasn't explicitly overridden it; the GUI can **reset any setting to its default** (Settings page: an "overridden" dot + a reset button; reset = delete the key so it falls back to the template — the GUI backend also prunes any saved-value-equals-default key on write, keeping the file sparse); an older FULL installed config still works (it merges to itself, a no-op). To change a value, set it in the installed file; to restore a default, DELETE its line (do NOT copy the template value in).

    **`lidar_enabled` is the one key with no template default**, and it is resolved by `robot_config_util.resolve_lidar_enabled()` from the config and NOTHING else — the `LIDAR_ENABLED` **environment variable is not read by the ROS2 stack** (removed 2026-08-31; as a "fallback when the yaml is silent" it silently overrode the operator's GUI toggle, running the stack GPS-only while the GUI said LiDAR on). An **absent key resolves to `false`** (`DEFAULT_LIDAR_ENABLED`) with a prominent startup WARNING naming the file, key and mode: absence means no LiDAR was ever recorded (the installer always writes it), a wrongly-OFF stack is coherent GPS-only, and a wrongly-ON one is the broken half-state. That default **must stay equal to the GUI JSON-schema default** (`gui/asserts/mower_config.schema.json`) — the settings backend prunes any value equal to its schema default, so a schema default of `true` would make "switch LiDAR ON" prune itself away and the toggle inert. `LIDAR_ENABLED` in `.env` still decides whether the `mowgli-lidar` CONTAINER starts, so the two can disagree the other way; `scan_deskew_node` (itself `use_lidar`-gated) warns after `scan_watchdog_period_s` if no scan ever arrives.

16. **Wheel-slip dig detection lives in `hardware_bridge_node`, and is the ONLY wheel-independent stuck-check.** Every other "am I stuck?" check on this robot measures the wheels, so none of them can see a *dig* (wheels spinning freely, chassis not moving, tyres carving a hole): the firmware anti-dig (`ANTIDIG_*` in `cpp_main.cpp`) compares encoder ticks to commanded travel — a spinning wheel yields the full tick count; FTC's stall cap (`ftc_stall.hpp`) reads its measured speed from `/wheel_odom` (`controller.odom_topic`) — the same encoders confirm the phantom motion, so it ramps to `speed_fast` and floors it, which is what digs; and `fusion_graph`'s slip veto (`slip_*` in `graph_params.hpp`) is **rotational only** (gyro yaw vs wheel yaw), so a straight-line dig passes with gyro ≈ 0 AND wheel-yaw ≈ 0 and gets dead-reckoned forward *through* the hole. The detector (`mowgli_hardware/dig_detector.hpp`, pure + unit-tested) compares encoder-claimed travel against the **GNSS-anchored fused pose** (`/odometry/filtered_map`) over `dig_window_s`; fresh RTK-Fixed `/gps/absolute_pose` displacement is an independent false-positive veto when it proves the chassis moved despite a lagging graph. Only when both disagree with the wheels does it **hard-stop on the wire, then drive a bounded reverse** (`dig_reverse_dist` / `dig_reverse_timeout_s`) that `on_cmd_vel` cannot override. It runs in the bridge because `~/cmd_vel` is twist_mux's MERGED output, so **one check covers every motion lane** (coverage, transit, docking, teleop); a controller-side check only ever sees its own lane, and docking is exactly a case it would miss. It **stands down** above `dig_max_pos_sigma` (RTK-Float can't tell slip from GNSS noise), above `dig_max_yaw_rate` (turning: encoders measure arc, the map measures chord, and the graph's own estimate degrades through a U-turn), and when the fused pose, raw RTK pose, gyro, or `cmd_vel` goes stale. **The turn exclusion reads the GYRO, never a wheel-derived yaw** — a one-wheel slip shows a huge *wheel* yaw rate with the chassis stationary, so a wheel-based exclusion would suppress exactly the dig this detector exists to catch. `dig_max_pos_sigma` is fed from the **GNSS receiver's** `horizontal_accuracy_m` under a confirmed RTK-Fixed solution (`DigTrustSigma`), NOT from fusion_graph's marginal. The graph marginal was tried and fails at ANY threshold: sampled over 90 s of live RTK-Fixed mowing (2026-08-24) it had p50 **0.574 m** / max 1.391 m while the receiver reported **0.014 m** and FTC tracked to 8–16 mm; 0.25 m blocked 57.6 % of ticks and 0.40 m still blocked 56.4 %. The fused pose remains the primary reference; raw RTK only vetoes a candidate when it independently confirms sufficient chassis displacement. This was added after the 2026-09-10 field bag recorded a healthy 0.20 m RTK displacement while the graph advanced only 0.05 m, causing a false hard-stop, reverse, and keepout under the robot. The bridge then publishes `~/dig_event`, which has TWO consumers, and **neither may ever mark a costmap cell**: nothing the dig pipeline does on its own may make a cell lethal/keepout under or around the robot, because the robot is standing ~0.2–0.3 m from the dig point (the bridge's reverse) and a keepout there refuses every plan FROM ITS OWN POSE. That is exactly how the old design failed — it stamped a session-scoped PENDING keepout (0.60 m polygon + the `keepout_obstacle_margin` band) into the mask: on 2026-09-10 (twice) and again on 2026-09-17 (bag part2-1920, dig at (-3.23, 11.01)) FTC kept following the ACTIVE coverage path — planned before the dig, straight through the new keepout, which FTC never reads since it only looks at the LOCAL costmap — wedged there for 35 s until the path-divergence guard cancelled the unit with the robot 0.27 m from the dig point, INSIDE the keepout; the re-plan grew from 9 to 10 sub-paths (the pending dig had become a coverage hole, which also breaks the plan determinism the resume cursor relies on), every transit was refused with START_OCCUPIED, `EscapeStartBlocked` is off by default, and after 5 attempts the area was given up with the robot stranded mid-lawn. **(1) `map_server` records an INERT PROPOSAL** (`add_obstacle_proposal`, `SOURCE_DIG`, evidence in its name): NOT in the keepout mask, NOT a NO_GO cell, NOT a hole returned by `get_mowing_area` (it is listed in `MapArea.proposed_obstacles` / `proposed_obstacle_info`, which only the GUI reads — so the coverage plan, its sub-path count and the resume cursor stay deterministic within a session) and NOT written to `areas.dat`. The operator accepts it in the GUI map page (`~/promote_obstacle` with `pending_id` — THAT applies it: mask + NO_GO + replan + persistence, exactly like a tracker obstacle, which also needs sign-off since `auto_promote_persistent_obstacles` defaults false) or rejects it (`~/discard_obstacle`). A dig outside every mowing area is logged, not proposed. The proposal polygon is sized to the PHYSICAL dig, never to the chassis: a regular octagon circumscribing the disc that covers both drive-wheel contact patches whatever the heading — `robot_config_util.dig_proposal_radius` = `hypot(wheel_track/2 + wheel_width/2, wheel_radius/2)` = **0.189 m** shipped, injected as `map_server.dig_proposal_radius`, grown by half the DigEvent's `map_distance` (rut length, capped at `kMaxDigSlipGrowthM` 0.10 m) and floored at `kMinDigProposalRadiusM` 0.10 m so the hole covers a map cell and stays clickable (`internal_helpers.hpp` `dig_proposal_radius` / `dig_proposal_polygon`). The old 0.60 m chassis-length, heading-biased box (`dig_obstacle_size`, `dig_keepout_polygon`, `kDigKeepoutBehindM`, the heading latch in `on_odom`) is REMOVED: it only existed because the polygon was stamped as a session keepout and therefore had to contain the body and stay out from under the reversed robot; as a proposal it just blanked out a big patch of lawn on every accept (and the GUI drew it as a TRIANGLE — `gui/web/src/types/map.ts` built unclosed rings and mapbox-gl-draw drops a ring's last position; fixed by `closeRing`). The body is counted ONCE, by the keepout band + `obstacle_margin` added around an ACCEPTED polygon. Because disc + band (≈0.20 + 0.276 m) reaches the spot the bridge reverses the robot to, `~/promote_obstacle{pending_id}` is **REFUSED while the robot centre is within `keepout_obstacle_margin` + one cell of the polygon** (`robot_inside_accepted_band`, clear message shown by the GUI) — refused, not deferred: a deferred accept is hidden state that would apply itself mid-mission. **(2) The anti re-dig protection of issue #500 (3 dig latches in 18.4 s inside 0.13 m — the controller drove straight back into the hole) lives in the coverage-FOLLOWING layer, as a session-scoped "dig skip zone"** (`mowgli_behavior/dig_skip.hpp`, pure + unit-tested; `behavior_tree_node` subscribes to `/hardware_bridge/dig_event` — VOLATILE on purpose, so a dig replayed from a previous session never becomes a zone — and keeps `BTContext::session_dig_points`, cleared by `EndSession`). On a dig DURING a `FollowCoveragePath` goal, `FollowStrip` **cancels that goal** (FTC must not keep pushing along the old path), waits for the bridge's bounded reverse to settle (`DigSettleStep`: robot still for 1 s, hard bound 8 s — the bridge publishes no "done" signal), then resumes the SAME unit at the first pose farther than `dig_skip_radius_m` past the dig point, reached by the existing blade-off transit (`trimUnitAt` + the structural gap guard), under `unit_resume.hpp`'s no-progress budget so a deterministic loop still ends. When dispatching or resuming ANY unit later in the session, poses inside a zone are skipped the same way: the goal is cut at the zone (`truncated_at_`), and reaching the cut is NOT the end of the unit. `dig_skip_radius_m` is DERIVED (`robot_config_util.dig_skip_radius` = the chassis circumscribed radius, 0.597 m shipped: inside it some part of the body — a drive wheel, or a front caster that then blocks the robot — can be over the hole) and injected by `full_system.launch.py`. Because nothing here touches a costmap, "plan from the robot's own pose" holds by construction (pinned by `test_map_server.cpp::DigEventLeavesTheKeepoutMaskAndTheCoverageHolesUntouched` and `test_follow_strip_dig.cpp`). KNOWN residual: the blade-off transit across a zone is a straight Smac plan, so the wheels may cross the rut once more (blade off, RPP instead of FTC's stall-cap ramp); a repeat dig is handled by the same recovery and bounded by the escalation below. `FollowStrip` also still does not spin the blade up on start when its first unit must be reached by a blade-off transit (`bladeSpinupBeforeFirstUnit`). **Operators tune all of this through ONE knob, `dig_sensitivity` (`off|low|medium|high`, template default `medium`)**, which `robot_config_util.dig_detector_params` expands into `dig_detect_enabled` / `dig_window_s` / `dig_min_wheel_dist` / `dig_progress_fraction` / `dig_escalate_count` and `mowgli.launch.py` injects AFTER `hardware_bridge.yaml`: `medium` is pinned equal to that file, `low` exists for tall/wet grass and sandy soil where a healthy robot slips enough to latch and then escalate to DIG_OBSTRUCTION, and is test-pinned to still latch every dig on record; `off` stops the HOST detector only. The firmware anti-dig is UNCHANGED and remains the un-bypassable backstop for the complementary case (BLOCKED wheels), which the host detector deliberately ignores via `dig_min_wheel_dist`. **How each side is MEASURED is load-bearing and was re-derived from the 2026-08-24 mow3 log (issue #500), where 33 sustained-spin episodes ground 69.7 m of tyre and only 4 latched, at a median 9.6 s after onset.** The wheel side is `OdometryPublisher::tyre_travelled()` = Σ max(|dL|,|dR|) — the WORST WHEEL, **not** the chassis-centre distance `(dL+dR)/2`: these digs are asymmetric by construction (issue #499 — a commanded turn radius below the half-track reverses the inner wheel), so the centre measure cancels them (it retained 42 % of the grinding tyre's travel, and exactly 0 % in a symmetric pivot), which left 13 of the 33 episodes unable to reach `dig_min_wheel_dist` inside one window and therefore **arithmetically undetectable**. The map side is the **NET DISPLACEMENT** from a per-window anchor, **never a sum of per-tick |steps|** — a sum is a path length that grows with estimator wander and with `dig_monitor_rate`, so raising the monitor rate made detection *worse*; measured at the five latches it swung 0.00–0.07 m against a ~0.09 m threshold while the robot's true motion was 0.13 m in 18 s. **On top of — and without changing — that per-event response, a repeat detector (`mowgli_hardware/dig_escalation.hpp`) latches `/hardware_bridge/dig_escalated` when `dig_escalate_count` (3) latches fall inside `dig_escalate_radius_m` (0.50 m) within `dig_escalate_window_s` (60 s), and the BT's `DigObstructionGuard` stops the mission there**: the 2026-09-04 capture latched 17 times against a physical object — five in 23 s inside a 6 cm square, then 5.5 min for 20 cm of progress — and neither the bounded reverse nor keeping the path out of the spot can break it, because the robot was not re-entering a mapped cell but being aimed back at the same object by the next planned manoeuvre. **The latch has exactly three exits** (field report 2026-09-14, a held robot could not be recovered): reaching the charger; the fused pose moving `kDigEscalationClearFactor` (2×) `dig_escalate_radius_m` away from the escalation point — the operator lifted it clear, or a HOME drove it out; or an OPERATOR-CONFIRMED clear through `~/clear_dig_escalation` (`std_srvs/Trigger`), which the bridge only honours once the fused pose has moved `dig_escalate_clear_distance_m` (0.50 m, floored at 0.30 m) from the escalation point — a stale fused pose makes it REFUSE rather than falsely succeed, so the override can shorten the automatic 1.00 m clearance but never replace the robot actually having been moved. While held the tree publishes state 1 (IDLE), which the firmware turns into a wheel+blade hard stop, so Play is refused and only HOME / manual modes act; HOME needs no clean-up step any more: the `~/discard_dig_keepouts_near_robot` service and the BT node `DiscardNearbyDigKeepouts` were REMOVED together with the keepouts they existed to drop (three same-spot latches used to stamp up to three 0.60 m keepouts around the robot, so the dock transit started in a lethal cell and never moved) — a dig never stamps anything now, so HOME can always plan from the robot's pose (`test_dig_obstruction_recovery.cpp`).

## What NOT to Do

- Do NOT add ROS1 patterns (rosserial, roscore, catkin) — this is ROS2 only
- Do NOT use FastRTPS — Cyclone DDS is required
- Do NOT mock the database/firmware in integration tests — use real interfaces
- Do NOT publish a `map→odom` (or `odom→base_footprint`) TF from any node other than `fusion_graph_node`. It is the sole, default, unconditional localizer and owns BOTH transforms — there is no `ekf_map_node` fallback and no `use_fusion_graph` launch arg (both were removed).
- Do NOT re-introduce slam_toolbox, Kinematic-ICP, or FusionCore. All three were tried and removed: slam_toolbox required a parallel TF tree + EWMA anchor + gating that was fragile across RTK-Float windows; K-ICP only gave a body-frame twist with no absolute-pose information; FusionCore's 22D UKF would not collapse σ_xy below ~30 cm even under RTK-Fixed. The factor-graph design (`fusion_graph`) is the replacement — LiDAR enters the same graph through validated scan-to-map XY observations.
- Do NOT make the dig detector (Invariant 16) trust a wheel-derived signal — that is the entire bug it exists to fix. Its primary reference MUST stay the GNSS-anchored fused pose, fresh RTK-Fixed raw displacement MUST retain its false-positive veto, and it MUST keep standing down above `dig_max_pos_sigma` rather than guessing under RTK-Float (a false dig hard-stops a healthy robot mid-mow). Do NOT re-measure the wheel side as chassis-centre distance or the map side as a sum of per-tick |steps| — both hid the failure the detector exists to catch (see Invariant 16). Do NOT re-point the turn exclusion at a wheel-derived yaw rate, and do NOT gate it on fusion_graph's covariance at all — neither `sqrt(max(var_xx, var_yy))` nor the major axis. That marginal reports metre-level sigma while the receiver reports centimetres, so no threshold works; the gate reads `/gps/status`. Do NOT weaken the firmware `ANTIDIG_*` gate to compensate for it either: the two cover different failures (blocked vs slipping wheels) and neither substitutes for the other. Do NOT make a detected dig apply ANYTHING to the map on its own again — not `areas.dat`, and NOT the keepout mask, the NO_GO classification or a `get_mowing_area` hole either: it is an inert PROPOSAL (Invariant 16) and applying it is the operator's call (`~/promote_obstacle` with `pending_id`). The session-scoped PENDING keepout that used to be stamped at the dig point stranded the robot mid-lawn twice (2026-09-10, 2026-09-17): the robot stands ~0.2–0.3 m from a fresh dig, so the keepout sat under it and every plan from its own pose was START_OCCUPIED. But do NOT drop the protection that keepout was there for either — issue #500's re-dig loop (3 latches in 18.4 s inside 0.13 m): it now lives in `FollowStrip`'s session dig skip zone (`mowgli_behavior/dig_skip.hpp` — cancel the active coverage goal on a dig, resume the same unit past the zone, skip the zone on every later unit). Do NOT move that protection back into a costmap, do NOT let `FollowStrip` keep its goal alive across a dig, do NOT hardcode `dig_skip_radius_m` (it derives from the chassis via `robot_config_util.dig_skip_radius`), and every new consumer of `MapServerNode::AreaEntry::obstacles` MUST skip `pending` entries.
- Do NOT let the start-pose escape (`EscapeStartBlocked`, issue #487, `mowgli_behavior/start_blocked_escape.hpp`) drift from its gates. It is a BT action node, not a hardware_bridge behaviour, precisely because it must stay OVERRIDABLE: it publishes on `/cmd_vel_nav` so collision_monitor still filters it and it rides the LOWEST twist_mux lane. Do NOT move it into the bridge (that layer only ever REDUCES motion — the dig escape of Invariant 16 lives there for the opposite reason), do NOT publish it past collision_monitor, do NOT fire it on anything but `IsCoverageStartBlocked`'s arming token, do NOT drop the verified-blade-off check or either bound, and do NOT replace the last-motion direction with a fixed one — a fixed reverse is correct mid-mow and drives DEEPER right after an undock, which is the reported case.
- Do NOT hardcode the robot's width (or any chassis dimension) in a consumer. `chassis_width` is operator-editable in the GUI, so every consumer must DERIVE from the merged config via `robot_config_util` (`chassis_footprint`, `chassis_half_width`, `chassis_circumscribed_radius`). On 2026-09-16 the chassis went 0.40 → 0.45 m: the Nav2 footprint and the inflation floor followed (they derive), while `navigation.launch.py` carried a literal `cw = 0.40` whose comment claimed it was "overwritten from the robot config" — an assignment that never existed. `coverage_server.robot_width` and `chassis_safety_inset` therefore stayed at 0.20, so every coverage plan routed the CENTRELINE 0.20 m from obstacle holes and the recorded line while the body reached 0.225 m — a plan 2.5 cm inside the robot, in collision before a single millimetre of tracking error. FTC, holding the line to 2 cm, drove exactly there and hit a MAPPED tree. **`chassis_safety_inset` and `obstacle_margin` are now SEPARATE concerns and must stay so.** `chassis_safety_inset` is BOUNDARY-only — how far inside the RECORDED LINE the outermost pass sits — and defaults to **0.0** because the operator recorded that perimeter by DRIVING it: it is the reachable limit, the ring rides on it, and every centimetre of inset is a band that never gets cut (0.2 m of it was the "stays too far from the hedges" complaint). `obstacle_margin` is the obstacle clearance, and with the inset at 0 it is the ONLY one — putting the ring on the line expands the planning field OUTWARD and `expandCellOutward` leaves the holes untouched. It is therefore FLOORED at `robot_config_util.planning_obstacle_margin_floor(rp)` (0.389 m shipped — see the next bullet), with the template AND GUI-schema default equal to it (`test_nav2_params.py::test_obstacle_margin_is_floored_at_the_body_half_width`, `::test_boundary_inset_and_obstacle_margin_are_separate_knobs`). Collapsing the two back into one value re-creates a knob with opposite requirements at each end, which is why no setting ever satisfied both.
- Do NOT restore FTC's `use_footprint_clearance: false` line model without re-reading why it went. Its `obstacle_body_half_width` is 0.12 m while the body reaches 0.275 m, so `obstacle_clearance_margin` had to stand in for the missing 0.155 m; thresholding at 253 then grows every obstacle by the inscribed radius (0.17 m) on top, making FTC demand 0.49 m from any lethal cell — including the hedge it is meant to mow along. Lowering the margin to get closer to edges removes the only thing compensating for a too-small planner inset. The footprint model samples the real chassis against true lethal cells (254): honest geometry, margin = real slack, no dependency on the inflation radius.
- Do NOT send blade commands without firmware safety checks
- Do NOT hardcode GPS coordinates, dock poses, or NTRIP credentials
- Do NOT count the robot's body more than ONCE per consumer — and know what each consumer's body model actually IS before growing an obstacle for it. **SmacPlanner2D** (transit) has NONE: it is a POINT check (centre cell ≥ INSCRIBED) and never tests the footprint polygon — the old yaml comment claiming a "per-footprint fallback" was wrong, that exists only in Hybrid-A*/Lattice.
